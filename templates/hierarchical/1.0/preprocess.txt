[TASK]
Follow these steps in order. Do not skip STEP 2.5 — it is the step that determines correct classification for schema-question cases, which are frequently phrased in ways that look like simple fact lookups on the surface.

STEP 1 — Read the DOCUMENT SCHEMA in full to understand its topics, structure, and hierarchy (sections, branches, nodes, and how they nest).

STEP 2 — Test for Atomic / Static Trivia vs. Knowledge Base Query:
Ask: "Is this a static, closed-form, single-fact lookup that requires NO knowledge base context at all?" Examples: "capital of Georgia", "what is the largest country", "who wrote Hamlet", "2 + 2".  Do NOT invent hypothetical expansions for atomic trivia — if it's simple trivia, treat it as such and move straight to `general-question`.

STEP 2.5 — Schema-Wide Search (mandatory for anything that isn't atomic trivia or small talk):
Search the ENTIRE schema for every node relevant to the query. Do not stop at the first match — a query may correspond to:
  (a) a single node and its children,
  (b) several children within one branch,
  (c) multiple unrelated branches scattered across different top-level sections,
  (d) no matching node at all.
Queries are often phrased in ways that obscure which of these applies — a narrow-sounding question can map to a whole branch; a yes/no-sounding question can be asking about a node's existence/location rather than a single fact; a comparison question can require synthesizing across branches with no direct link between them in the schema. Actually search before assuming from phrasing alone.

Then judge: does answering require describing WHAT EXISTS and WHERE in the knowledge base (a node's contents, several nodes' contents, an existence/location check, a comparison or overlap across branches) — or does it require the substantive CONTENT of one already-identified concept, definition, or process?

STEP 3 — Categorize into exactly ONE type, using the result of STEP 2.5:

1. `general-question` — ONLY for:
   - Greetings, small talk, polite chatter.
   - Assistant identity/capabilities questions.
   - Atomic/static trivia and quick lookups (STEP 2), with zero knowledge-base relevance.
   -> ALWAYS set `retrieval_query` to "".

2. `schema-question` — the query's answer is fundamentally about the STRUCTURE or INVENTORY of the
   knowledge base, as determined in STEP 2.5:
   - Contents of one node/branch.
   - Contents aggregated across several scattered nodes.
   - Existence or location checks ("do I have notes on X", "where should I look for Y").
   - Comparisons, overlaps, or pattern-finding across branches.
   - High-level overviews or topic inventories of the whole base or a large slice of it.
   This category applies REGARDLESS of how narrow, specific, or fact-like the query sounds on the
   surface — the deciding factor is always what STEP 2.5 found, not the query's wording.

3. `fact-question` — the query's answer is the substantive CONTENT of one already-identified
   concept, tool, process, or definition. Retrieval targets deep content about one thing, not a
   structural map of several things.

STEP 4 — Rewrite as `improved_query`: a clean, natural-language question for a downstream LLM.
For `general-question`, return a cleaned-up version of the original.

STEP 5 — Generate `retrieval_query`:
- For `fact-question` / `schema-question`: a keyword-dense search query optimized for vector
  retrieval, drawing on the schema terms/paths identified in STEP 2.5.
- For `general-question`: strictly "".

STEP 6 — Write `reasoning`: state explicitly which schema node(s) you found in STEP 2.5 (name them,
even if there are several unrelated ones, or none), whether each is a container/branch or a single
leaf item, and how that determined the category. Do not write vague reasoning like "high-level
overview of the user" — name the actual paths.
